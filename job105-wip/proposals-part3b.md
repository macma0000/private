`## Chapter 16: The Fixations Themselves`

[A lean only shades what enthusiasm or caution does, but a lean changes what detachment does. Leaning toward caution, a detached person is able to notice a great deal. Leaning toward enthusiasm, a detached person turns away from whatever would disturb their peace, and notices less and less. A fear is handled by the fixation a person holds in the realm the fear is about. The fixations differ most in how much attention each gives the detached phase, where fears meet their fates. One endeavor, followed through by an enthusiastic person, a cautious person, and a detached person leaning each way, shows the differences plainly. A fixation begins in infancy. In each realm separately, one of three ways of meeting pleasure and pain comes to predominate: going toward what is pleasant, trying to avoid what is painful, or watching without doing either. Once a child can foresee pleasure and pain, the child's reactions become strategies, and the strategies go on long after the helplessness they answered is over. A fixation then runs as a habit, below the level where anything is decided, so a person cannot find their own fixation by looking for it. Undoing a fixation means bringing it back to where a person can decline it, not replacing it with a better orientation. A fixation lasts a lifetime because each thought arises out of what earlier thoughts have laid down.]

`### The range of detachment`

> For enthusiasm and caution a lean shades the orientation without altering what it does.

**What it previews:** in the chapter's order: detachment's two leans, which fixation handles a fear and how each treats the detached phase, the four sketches, where a fixation comes from in infancy, strategies outliving helplessness, habit and why a person cannot find their own fixation, what undoing a fixation means, and why a fixation lasts.

`## Chapter 17: The Health Axis`

[The health axis reflects how much a person notices, and Riso and Hudson's nine Levels of Development mark the places along the axis, from top to bottom. ### Chapter 18: Falling and Rising

**14. Chapter 18's opening**

`## Chapter 18: Falling and Rising`

[In a fall, what goes down is how much of their own condition a person notices, and the noticing goes down a very little at a time, with each blocked fear. Discomfort blocking is the usual way down in Levels 1 to 6. Falling adds stress, stress lowers how much a person can bear, and so each blocked fear makes the next fear more likely to be blocked. Nobody watches themselves go down. Most people still stop somewhere in the middle levels, because a fall is slow and because the consequences of a fall push back. Someone else can help a person far down, by steadying the person and by giving the person the truth about their condition a little at a time. Urging a person to face a fear does harm, because urging is pressure, and pressure lowers how much the person can bear. A climb happens where a fall does, in the detached phase, when a blocked fear comes back into view and is faced. Before a buried fear can be faced, the person has to bear the fear of looking at the buried fear. The capacity to bear discomfort, which facing takes, can be built, and a skill for handling fear can be taught. A steadiness trained in one realm is often mistaken for the top of the levels. Trying harder does not change a person, because a person's effort goes only into what the person already notices.]

`### What actually descends`

> One thing moves on this axis, and it is not how much a person suffers or how hard they are trying.

**What it previews:** in the chapter's order: noticing as what falls, discomfort blocking as the usual way down, stress and the fall feeding itself, nobody watching themselves go down, why most people stop in the middle, how another person helps and why urging harms, rising and the fear of looking, the capacity and the skill, the counterfeit summit, and why trying harder does not change a person. *The pain of life not working* and *intervener* are explained later in the chapter, so the opening uses neither.
