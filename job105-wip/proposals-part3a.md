`## Chapter 13: Where the Inner World Came From`

[A person's inner world goes back to animals developing brains that carry a picture of their surroundings. To predict what another animal will do, an animal can ask what it would do itself, and asking turns the ability to picture inward. A person's self comes from that ability turned inward. The self is real, but the self is not an object, and looking inward never finds the self. A mind taking itself as its subject is what most sharply separates the psychic realm from the physical realm. A person's fixation cannot be found by looking inward either, and shows instead in what the person does and in what other people notice. Detachment is directed at a person's own reactions rather than at things. So detachment has two faces, and any way out of a fixation runs through detachment. Turned outward, the ability to picture is language, which lets what one person works out reach other people. A person needs the ability to picture in order to notice their own fixation, so the way out cannot be to quiet the mind.]

`## Chapter 14: Underneath the Cycle`

[Every thought a person has takes about a second, and moves from phase to phase through three passages: the arising, the development and the ending. The three phases are named after the three orientations. A person's orientation in a realm is the phase of a thought the person prefers to be in. A thought ends in the detached phase, where a fear the thought raised is either settled at the caution end of the detached phase or left unsettled at the enthusiasm end. Each thought lays something down, and the next thought arises out of what was laid down. So a want or a fear that seems to last a long time is really raised again in thought after thought. A disappointment arises when an outcome is settled and a want cannot be satisfied. Every gratification wears off, so seeking gratification leads to one useless change of behavior after another. An action can also come before any thought, or after a thought cut short. In each realm, a person's fixation shows in which phase of each thought gets most of the person's attention. Where the attention goes affects how many fears a person raises and what becomes of the fears.]

`## Chapter 15: What Becomes of a Fear`

[Choosing to leave a fear alone and no longer noticing a fear at all are easy to confuse, and are different. Of the four things that can become of a fear in the detached phase, only blocking leaves a person noticing less than before. A fear is usually turned away from in panic only after the fear has come up repeatedly and grown, until one time the fear is more than a person can bear. Panic does the turning away, not the person, so nothing is decided. Afterward, anything that comes near the fear raises a fresh anxiety, and turning away from the anxiety keeps the fear out of view. While a matter stays unresolved, a person can work through each fear about the matter and still be left uneasy, without anything being turned away from. Unease that lasts comes from ignored aversions, from blocked aversions, and from being less able to handle life than others are, and a person with lasting unease looks for one distraction after another.]
