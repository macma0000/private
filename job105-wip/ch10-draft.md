## Chapter 10: Integration and Disintegration

The classical Enneagram gives each type a direction of integration and a direction of disintegration, and draws both as arrows on two shapes, the triangle and the hexad. Both directions and both shapes follow from three orientations in two realms. When stress is low, a person shifts a little toward the types a step ahead of their own type, in one realm or in both, which is integration. Under stress, a person shifts a little toward the types a step behind instead, which is disintegration. The triangle joins the three aligned types, each a step ahead of the next in both realms, and the hexad joins the six mixed types, each a step ahead of the next in one realm, with the two realms taking turns. Twelve more one-step relations run between the mixed types and the aligned types, and the classical figure draws none of the twelve, though a person seems to shift a little toward those types too. A person who holds their orientation in a realm less strongly has some of the orientation a step ahead available, and so comes to resemble the type ahead in that realm while keeping their own type.

In the classical Enneagram, every type has a direction of integration, which a person is said to move in when stress is low, and a direction of disintegration, which a person is said to move in under stress. The arrows for both directions run along two shapes inside the circle: a triangle joining 3, 6 and 9, and a six-pointed figure joining the other six. Which type connects to which is simply given. No reason has ever been offered for that particular order.

Both shapes fall out of the grid.

### The types ahead and behind

Integration is the forward direction around the cycle of orientations: a shift toward the orientation one step forward. One step forward in a realm is what ahead means. The types a person integrates toward are therefore the types ahead of them.

Disintegration is the same lines read backward. Behind replaces ahead throughout, each realm shifting a little toward the orientation behind it rather than the one ahead, and the two shapes close in the reverse order. The classical figure marks both directions on the same lines, for the same reason.

Every type has three types ahead of it and three behind it. Two lie a single step ahead, each one place further round the cycle in one realm only. One lies a double step ahead, one place further round in both realms. A step ahead is a relation between two types. A step ahead is not a move a person makes.

The Nine is detached in both realms. The orientation one place ahead of detachment is enthusiasm. So the type a single step ahead of the Nine in the physical realm is the Seven, and the type a single step ahead in the psychic realm is the Two. The type a double step ahead is the Three, who is enthusiastic in both realms. So a Nine integrates toward the Seven or the Two by a single step, and toward the Three by a double one. Read backward, the types a single step behind the Nine are the Five in the physical realm and the Four in the psychic, and the type a double step behind is the Six.

### Why the two shapes close

Take the aligned types first. A single step in one realm leaves the other where it was, so it breaks the alignment; only the double step preserves it. The three aligned types reach one another by exactly that step, each one double ahead of the next, and the path through the three comes back to where it started. That closed path corresponds to the classical triangle.

The mixed types hold two different orientations, and there the single step does the work. Advance one realm and one of two things happens: either the advancing realm arrives where the other already stands, and the type becomes aligned, or it does not, and the type stays mixed. With three orientations and the two realms already differing, exactly one of them is the realm that would arrive. So of the two types a single step ahead of any mixed type, one sits on the diagonal and one does not.

Follow the one that does not. The One is enthusiastic physically and cautious psychically; advancing the physical realm would make it cautious in both, so it is the psychic realm that steps, from caution to detachment, and the One arrives at the Seven. Now the realm that has just moved is the one that would arrive if it moved again — the Seven's psychic orientation is detachment, which is one step from enthusiasm, and enthusiasm is already the Seven's physical orientation. So the other realm takes the next step. The two realms alternate, strictly, and never advance twice in a row.

![The hexad as a chain of single steps: 1, 7, 5, 8, 2, 4 and back to 1, each arrow colored by which realm advances — teal for the physical, purple for the psychic — alternating strictly.](hexad-cycle.svg)

Each step advances one realm by one place on a cycle of three, and the realms take turns. After six steps each realm has gone once around its own cycle and every type is back where it began: 1, 7, 5, 8, 2, 4, and back to the One. Six is not a number chosen to fit. It is what the alternation forces, and the order of the six is forced with it.

![Integration in the two families, side by side: the triad's every step advances both realms at once — paired arrows — while the hexad advances one realm at a time, alternating.](triad-hexad-cycles.svg)

Those two closed paths correspond to the two shapes the classical figure draws, and they were arrived at from three orientations in two realms with nothing borrowed from the figure itself.

![The classical nine-pointed figure redrawn: the hexad as a hexagon of the six mixed types with the aligned triangle inside, and dashed lines for the twelve single-ahead relations that cross between the two groups.](enneagram-hexagon.svg)

### The relations the figure does not draw

Every type has two types a single step ahead of it, so there are eighteen single-ahead relations among the nine. Six run between mixed types. Those are the hexad, and the figure draws them. The other twelve cross between the mixed types and the diagonal — each mixed type has one aligned type a step ahead of it, and each aligned type has two mixed types a step ahead of it. They are the dashed lines above, and the classical figure draws none of them.

A crossing relation is the same move as a hexad step: one realm advancing a single place, the other held. It differs only in where it lands.

Relational position was given earlier as a fact about two people, something felt from one side or the other. A relation running forward is also the direction along which one of the two stands to the other as the next orientation stands to the one before it. Nothing about a person changes type; the pair a person holds is what they are. What the types ahead mark is where that person's own two orientations hold them fast, and as that grip loosens they come to resemble those types more closely. When stress is low, a person also shifts a little toward those types for a while. The classical arrows describe both changes: the lasting one as the grip loosens, and the passing one when stress is low.

If the arrows report the two changes, then the two changes should hold wherever the relation holds. A One has the Six a single step ahead in the physical realm as surely as the Seven a single step ahead in the psychic, and integrates toward the one as toward the other. A Six has the Four and the Five ahead as well as the Nine. The classical system cannot say this, having no relation at all between a type and the diagonal. It is set down here to be tested. It accords with observation, and observation is not yet evidence.

### Integration, type by type

Being less caught in a realm means holding the orientation in that realm less strongly. Becoming less caught shows up the same way in either realm.

The orientation a person holds in a realm has a center. A person who is deeply caught stays at the center, because a deeply caught person cannot easily move off the center.

Health makes the center easier to leave. A person who can leave the center is found a little behind the center at some times, and a little ahead of the center at others. Such a person moves either way, as the matter at hand calls for. A person who can leave the center goes ahead of the center more often than behind it, because a fixation holds a person back from the orientation ahead. The person cannot easily bear what the next phase would bring. A less caught person can bear more of what the next phase would bring, and so goes ahead more easily. Being less caught does not move the center. What being less caught changes is how easily a person leaves the center and returns.

A less caught person can bear more of what the next orientation brings, and so can anyone when stress is low. Where a person is enthusiastic, the person can bear the examining, even when the examining shows that the hoped-for reward may not come. Where a person is cautious, the person can bear losing control, and lets fears be settled. Where a person is detached, the person can bear a want arising, and the fears a want can bring.

Ahead of the center, some of the orientation ahead is available. The orientation ahead, paired with the orientation the person holds in their other realm, gives the type ahead in the realm where the person is less caught. So a person becoming less caught comes to resemble the type ahead, while still holding the same two orientations.

A One less caught in the physical realm can weigh how hard a thing will actually be before deciding what ought to be done about it. The type ahead of a One in that realm is the Six. A One less caught in the psychic realm can take a report about their own standards as news rather than dismissing it. The type ahead there is the Seven.

A Two less caught in the physical realm notices what is there, and can then act on what a situation requires. The type ahead of a Two in that realm is the Three. A Two less caught in the psychic realm can notice how worn down the giving leaves them, rather than moving on to the next person who needs something. The type ahead there is the Four.

A Three less caught in the physical realm can stay with an attempt that fell short instead of turning to the next one. The type ahead of a Three in that realm is the Eight. A Three less caught in the psychic realm notices when an achievement fails to settle the question of their own worth. The type ahead there is the One. A Three less caught in both realms comes to resemble the Six, the type a double step ahead.

A Four less caught in the physical realm notices the demands of physical life, and can then take them up. The type ahead of a Four in that realm is the One. A Four less caught in the psychic realm can let the analysis come to an end. The type ahead there is the Nine.

A Five less caught in the physical realm can tell when enough has been understood. The type ahead of a Five in that realm is the Nine. A Five less caught in the psychic realm can stop rationing feeling and involvement as though feeling and involvement would run out. The type ahead there is the Eight.

A Six less caught in the physical realm can tell when enough has been checked. The type ahead of a Six in that realm is the Four. A Six less caught in the psychic realm can let a worry come to a stop instead of compounding. The type ahead there is the Five. A Six less caught in both realms comes to resemble the Nine, the type a double step ahead.

A Seven less caught in the physical realm can stay with what has gone wrong long enough to understand it. The type ahead of a Seven in that realm is the Five. A Seven less caught in the psychic realm can undergo a feeling rather than turn it into the next arrangement. The type ahead there is the Three.

An Eight less caught in the physical realm can take a report that the directness landed as domination as news rather than dismissing it. The type ahead of an Eight in that realm is the Two. An Eight less caught in the psychic realm can take the time to understand a person before engaging them wholeheartedly. The type ahead there is the Six.


A Nine less caught in the physical realm notices what is there, and can then want something and act on it. The type ahead of a Nine in that realm is the Seven. A Nine less caught in the psychic realm notices a conflict and can then enter it. The type ahead there is the Two. A Nine less caught in both realms comes to resemble the Three, the type a double step ahead.

### Disintegration, type by type

Stress lowers what a person can bear, and under stress a person looks more like the orientation behind their own. The shift lasts while the stress does, and the person's type does not change. Stress cuts short the work a person's own orientation does, and the person acts on what is in hand without that work. Where a person is enthusiastic, the person under stress seems to stop short of acting, and withdraws. Where a person is cautious, the person stops weighing, and acts recklessly on a fear that was never weighed. Where a person is detached, the person seems to stop settling the fears that arrive, so the same fears come back, and the person worries.

A One under stress in the physical realm seems to stop short of acting on what ought to be done, and withdraws from the task. The type behind a One in that realm is the Four. A One under stress in the psychic realm stops weighing whether a thing is proper, and acts at once on the fear of being in the wrong. The type behind there is the Three.

A Two under stress in the physical realm seems to stop settling the practical fears that arrive, so the same fears about time, money and arrangements come back, and the Two worries. The type behind a Two in that realm is the Eight. A Two under stress in the psychic realm seems to stop short of reaching out to people, and the giving stops. The type behind there is the Nine.

A Three under stress in the physical realm seems to stop short of the next attempt, and nothing new gets started. The type behind a Three in that realm is the Two. A Three under stress in the psychic realm seems to stop short of the next achievement meant to settle their worth, and the drive goes flat. The type behind there is the Seven. A Three under stress in both realms comes to resemble the Nine, the type a double step behind.

A Four under stress in the physical realm seems to stop settling the practical fears that arrive, so the demands of physical life come back again and again as worries. The type behind a Four in that realm is the Six. A Four under stress in the psychic realm stops weighing a feeling, and acts on it at once. The type behind there is the Two.

A Five under stress in the physical realm stops weighing, and acts recklessly on whatever is in hand before understanding it. The type behind a Five in that realm is the Seven. A Five under stress in the psychic realm seems to stop settling the fears about people that arrive, so the same fears come back, and the Five worries. The type behind there is the Six.

A Six under stress in the physical realm stops checking, and acts recklessly on a danger that was never weighed. The type behind a Six in that realm is the One. A Six under stress in the psychic realm stops weighing a worry about people, and acts on the worry at once. The type behind there is the Eight. A Six under stress in both realms comes to resemble the Three, the type a double step behind.

A Seven under stress in the physical realm seems to stop short of the next plan, and withdraws. The type behind a Seven in that realm is the Nine. A Seven under stress in the psychic realm seems to stop settling the fears that arrive, so the same fears come back, and the Seven worries. The type behind there is the One.

An Eight under stress in the physical realm stops weighing where the weakness is, and acts recklessly on a threat that was never weighed. The type behind an Eight in that realm is the Three. An Eight under stress in the psychic realm seems to stop short of engaging people, and withdraws. The type behind there is the Five.

A Nine under stress in the physical realm seems to stop settling the practical fears that arrive, so the same fears come back, and the Nine worries. The type behind a Nine in that realm is the Five. A Nine under stress in the psychic realm seems to stop settling the fears about people that arrive, and the Nine worries about the people around them. The type behind there is the Four. A Nine under stress in both realms comes to resemble the Six, the type a double step behind.

### Reading it from either end

The correspondence can be read from either end. Taken with the classical figure as primary, the two-realm model recovers its two cycles, and their order, out of five elements — two realms and three orientations — none of which came from the figure. Taken with the model as primary, the hexad that the older system asserts is what a single forward step looks like when the realms take turns, and the triangle is what it looks like when they move together.
