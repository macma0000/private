### Chapter 3: The Two Realms

#### 3.1 The chapter's opening

**Where it goes:** under the heading `## Chapter 3: The Two Realms`, before the chapter's first sentence.

**In place:**

> It asks you to hold it more lightly — with curiosity rather than defense — and to become genuinely interested in what lies just beneath it.

`## Chapter 3: The Two Realms`

[In the physical world, what happens can be checked by anyone, and more effort usually brings a result closer. The inner world belongs to each person alone, and what a person believes affects what the person experiences. Trying hard to fall asleep does the opposite of what effort does in the physical world, because the trying is what keeps a person awake. Neither world is more real than the other. ### Chapter 4: The Three Orientations

#### 4.1 The chapter's opening

**Where it goes:** under the heading `## Chapter 4: The Three Orientations`, before the chapter's first sentence.

**In place:**

> Recall a time you tried to fix an inner state by outer means, and a time you tried to move the world by wanting. The two failures have entirely different textures.

`## Chapter 4: The Three Orientations`

[A person's wants and fears can arise from being drawn toward something or from being drawn away from it. A person can be enthusiastic, cautious or detached toward a realm, and each of the three orientations gives the person a real competence that is part of why the person keeps the orientation. What a person notices, and what the person overlooks, depends on the orientation the person holds. Intuition is noticing later something that was never noticed at the time. People holding the three orientations also respond differently when something goes wrong. Each orientation comes with something a person cannot easily bear: disappointment for an enthusiastic person, losing control for a cautious person, and the discomfort that fear leaves behind for a detached person. When a fear grows to be more than a detached person can bear, the person can end up in denial.]

> Only the same three orientations are possible toward the content of each realm.

**What it previews:** in the chapter's order: where wants and fears come from; the three orientations and their competences; noticing and overlooking; intuition; what each does when something goes wrong; what each cannot easily bear; and what a detached person does with a fear, as far as denial. Part 1's opening says only that each way is good at something, overlooks something and cannot easily bear something; this opening says why a person keeps an orientation, what sets the noticing, and names what each cannot easily bear.
